Infrastructure for
an economy that
already exists

South Africa's informal traders are not waiting for permission to trade. They are trading on crates, on blankets and out of car boots. THATHA sells them the one thing they cannot improvise: a professional, portable trading platform at a price they can reach.

Read this first.

THATHA is at pre-production stage. This page sets out the intended business model and the design targets it is being engineered against. It contains no trading history, no revenue figures and no completed deployments, because there are none yet. Every number shown is a target or a planning assumption, and each is labelled as such.

The thesis

Four things that
make this work

01

Cost is the whole moat

Trading structures exist already — they are simply priced for businesses that have capital. The engineering target is a high-volume build cost near R300 per Basic unit, achieved by replacing mechanical hardware with a slot-and-pin construction. If that target holds, the addressable market changes shape entirely.

02

The first sale is not the business

Umbrellas, cooler inserts, shelving, lighting, advertising panels, QR holders and replacement parts all attach to the same frame. A trader who upgrades a trade buys accessories, not a new unit — which turns a one-off purchase into a recurring relationship.

03

Institutions buy in hundreds

Municipal formalisation budgets, NGO development funding and corporate enterprise-development spend all need visible, countable, auditable interventions. A portable unit with an asset register is far easier to procure than a permanent structure.

04

Every unit is a media surface

Detachable sponsor panels make each deployed unit a brandable surface in a high-footfall location. That is a third revenue line that grows with the fleet rather than with the sales team.

Risks

What could go wrong

The R300 build cost does not hold

This is the primary risk. The target assumes high-volume production, a simplified slot-and-pin construction and local material sourcing. If validated costs land materially higher, the retail ladder moves up and the addressable trader market narrows. Prototype costing is deliberately the first milestone for exactly this reason.

Institutional sales cycles are long

Municipal and corporate procurement can run over multiple budget cycles. The consumer and small-trader channel exists partly to generate revenue and product evidence while institutional pipelines mature.

Durability in real conditions is unproven

A unit that is cheap but fails in a season destroys trust and creates warranty cost. The pilot exists to surface failure modes early, and the repairable design limits the cost of any single failure to a replaceable panel.

Competitors can copy a simple design

The construction is deliberately simple, so it is not defensible on engineering alone. Defensibility is intended to come from manufacturing cost at volume, the accessory ecosystem, brand, and institutional relationships — not from the joint design.
