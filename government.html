Questions your
supply-chain team will ask

What stage is the product at?

Pre-production. The design is defined and the range is specified, but unit costing depends on prototype sign-off, bill-of-materials validation and manufacturer quotation. We would rather say that upfront than quote a number we cannot hold.

Can you hold the indicative prices shown on this site?

No. Every figure published here is a planning target to support budgeting conversations. A binding price requires a formal quotation against a defined specification, unit count and delivery schedule.

Are the units manufactured locally?

Local manufacture and assembly is a core design constraint, not an afterthought — the slot-and-pin construction exists specifically so units can be built and repaired without imported proprietary hardware. Supplier appointments are confirmed per programme.

How are units maintained after handover?

Through a spares pipeline rather than a warranty-return model. Panels and pins are standard parts, so a damaged unit is repaired in place. Programme agreements set the spares volume and response time.

What documentation do we get for reporting?

A unit register with asset identifiers, delivery evidence, beneficiary records and training attendance. The exact pack is agreed at contracting so it matches your audit and donor requirements.

Can units be relocated?

Yes — that is the point of a portable unit. Units can be reallocated between sites or traders without demolition or capital loss, which is a material advantage over fixed structures.
